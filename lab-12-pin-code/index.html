Pin Code

Idea

What if a PIN code was a tune? This lock is a music box. The pins on its drum are the code: place them, turn the crank, and the comb plays exactly what you set. Only the right melody springs the drawer, and the key inside is, of course, in the right key.

Controls

  • Tap a square on the drum to add or remove a pin. Each pin glows in the colour of the tooth it will pluck.
  • Drag the crank handle round to play by hand, or press Play and let the spring do it. Drag the drum up or down to spin it.
  • Match the four notes under "The tune", in order. Gaps between notes are fine. The lamps on the box count your correct notes, and a wrong note rattles the latch.
  • Keyboard: Space plays or stops, arrow keys turn the drum one step, 1 to 6 add or remove a pin in the front row, C clears the pins, N picks a new tune.

Inspired by

  • 0xbigturk's isometric calculator in SVG: an object you can actually use, where every key sinks and the screen really works.
  • Music box and melody-lock puzzles in point-and-click adventures and escape rooms.
  • Cylinder music boxes, where every pin is a note, the steel comb is the instrument and the drum is the score.

For game UI

Let the puzzle object be the interface. When the lock itself shows its state (lamps that count, a tooth that flexes, a latch that rattles on a wrong note), players read progress from the thing in their hands instead of from a separate HUD. The same approach works for safes, radios, terminals and any in-world device: every part reacts, and the feedback lives where the player is looking.
