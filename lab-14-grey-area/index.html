Lab #14 · Motion · 18 Oct 2026

Grey Area

Idea

What if you could see a level's whole production at once? A small platformer runs in a cave full of glowing mushrooms. Ship it plays the art passes in order (shapes, colour, light, effects) and the greybox turns into the final scene in a few seconds. The lens, the grey area, keeps the blockout visible underneath: grid, measurements, jump arcs and markers. The HUD on top never changes, and a live meter tells you whether it is still readable at every stage.

Controls

  • Drag on the game to move the lens. Scroll, pinch or use the Lens slider to resize it. Lens on/off hides it.
  • Shapes, Colour, Light and FX switch each art pass on or off. Each one animates in from left to right.
  • Ship it plays every pass in order. Back to grey rewinds to the blockout.
  • HUD backing adds a dark plate behind the HUD. Watch the contrast meter change.
  • Keyboard (with the game focused): arrow keys move the lens, + and − resize it, 1 to 4 toggle passes, S ships, G goes back to grey.

Inspired by

  • A PixVerse video that shows the same game scene as greybox and as final art, with the same HUD on top of both.
  • Level design blockouts: untextured grey geometry with measurements, used to test layout and timing before any art.
  • X-ray and inspection lenses in editors and art tools, which show the layer underneath without leaving the current view.

For game UI

A HUD is usually signed off on one screenshot of final art, but players see it over every kind of scene. Testing it from the greybox onwards catches problems early: a white HUD with a light shadow reads well in a dark cave and fails on mid-grey blockout, and the reverse happens in snow or desert levels. Sampling the pixels behind the HUD and showing a live contrast ratio turns that into a number the team can track at every milestone, and a backing plate or adaptive outline is the fix you can try on the spot.
