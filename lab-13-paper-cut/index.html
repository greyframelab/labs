Lab #13 · UI · 17 Oct 2026

Paper Cut

Idea

What if your guild name cut its own crest? The name is cut into a folded sheet of paper, one snip per letter: vowels punch holes, consonants notch the folds, numbers nick the edge, and the first letter picks the paper and the shape of its rim. Unfold it and the name becomes an emblem. Type the same name tomorrow, on any device, and you get exactly the same crest.

Controls

  • Type in "Guild name" (letters, numbers and spaces, up to 16). Each new letter lands as a cut and the paper chips fall away.
  • Points sets how many times the sheet is folded, from 3 to 8 points. Same name, new symmetry.
  • Fold and Unfold show the folded sheet and open it crease by crease. Light up holds it against a lamp.
  • Hover or tap a hole, or a letter in the cut list, to see which letter made it. Drag the paper to spin it. Random name tries another guild.
  • Keyboard: arrow keys spin, F folds or unfolds, L lights it up (with the paper focused).

Inspired by

  • SIGILIZERSIXHUNDRED by niftymonkee: a word that turns into a sigil, letter by letter.
  • Papel picado, the Mexican folk art of cutting patterns into stacked tissue paper for festival banners.
  • Folded paper snowflakes: fold a sheet a few times, make a few snips, and the symmetry does the rest.

For game UI

Emblem editors usually hand players a menu of icons, colours and frames. A deterministic generator turns that around: the name the player already chose becomes the art. Every guild gets a unique crest with no extra steps, and any client can rebuild it from the text alone, so there is no image to store or sync. Showing each letter's cut as it lands keeps the system readable, so players can steer the result instead of rerolling it. The same idea works for player badges, spell runes, ship liveries or shareable seeds.
