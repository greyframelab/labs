Lab #15 · Creative Coding · 19 Oct 2026

Moonlighting

Idea

What if every building on the pier had a second job? You build a tiny harbour in daylight: a fish stall, a boat shed, a cargo crane, a net shed, a water tower. Drag the clock past sunset and each one opens its night business at its own hour. Its sign flips and the building rebuilds itself: the fish stall becomes a noodle bar, the boat shed a cinema, the crane a swing ride, the net shed a café under fairy lights, and the water tower an observatory whose telescope follows the moon. The crowd that turns up depends on who your neighbours are.

Controls

  • Pick a building in the dock (or press 1 to 5), then tap an empty tile to build it. Tap a building with the same type selected to remove it, or with another type to replace it.
  • Drag the clock, or use the arrow keys on it, to change the time. The coloured ticks on the clock are each building's opening hour.
  • Sunset and Sunrise play the hours in between.
  • Drag the scene to look around. Scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Neighbours matter: a noodle bar next to a cinema brings more people, a swing ride next to the observatory drowns the stars.

Inspired by

  • Midnight Moments by @zoadle, a diorama game where the scene you build comes alive when night falls. The principle taken here: the switch to night as the reward for building.
  • Life sims and farming games where every shop keeps its own opening hours, so the time of day changes what the world offers.
  • Harbour and market streets where the same stalls change trade after dark.

For game UI

Time of day is a state, and the world's schedule should be readable before it happens. Putting every building's opening hour on the clock as a tick tells players what will change and when. A sign that flips makes a state change impossible to miss, even at a distance. And when neighbours change a number, a toast over the pair that says why turns a hidden rule into something players can plan around.
