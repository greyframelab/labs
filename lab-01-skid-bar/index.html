Skid Bar

What if the scrollbar had tyres?

The bar on the right is a race track and the car is your scroll position. Scroll fast and it accelerates. Brake hard and the tail steps out. Reverse and it pulls a handbrake turn. Every slide leaves rubber on the track.

S1

Sector 1 · Main straight

Speed is scroll velocity

The number in the timing panel reads how fast the page is moving: one km/h for every 14 pixels per second. A long flick on a trackpad or a hard swipe on a phone will push it past 300.

The timer started the moment you left the grid. Crossing the yellow line on the track closes the sector.

S2

Sector 2 · Hairpin

Flick back up

The car has no reverse gear. Change scroll direction and it spins 180° on the handbrake, swings wide and paints a loop of rubber on the asphalt.

Try it here, then carry on down. Turning around costs time.

S3

Sector 3 · Chicane

Brake lights on

Stop a fast scroll suddenly and the rear lamps flare while the back of the car slides out. Short, sharp flicks make it twitch through the chicane.

You can also grab the car or tap the track to jump. It works like any scrollbar, but the stewards will delete the lap for exceeding track limits.

Lab #01 · UI · 3 Oct 2026

Skid Bar

Idea

What if the scrollbar had tyres? A pun on the scroll "bar" turned into a track, where your reading speed becomes driving.

Controls

  • Scroll, swipe or use the keyboard to drive.
  • Reverse direction to spin the car.
  • Drag the car or tap the track to jump. That deletes the current lap.
  • Back to the top resets the grid.

Inspired by

  • Mario Kart drift sparks: feedback that grows while you hold an input.
  • Bruno Simon's portfolio, where you drive a car to navigate the site.
  • F1 timing screens: purple for the fastest sector, yellow for slower.

For game UI

Garages, inventories and level select screens are long scrolls. Give the scroll weight (momentum, a reaction when it stops) and leave a trace of where the player paused. Fading skid marks work as a free heatmap of attention.
