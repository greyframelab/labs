Burst Mode

Idea

What if you packed your own firework, star by star, and the sky drew it back? A real aerial shell is a paper ball packed by hand with little pellets called stars, and where each star sits decides where it flies. Here the editor is that shell. You place glowing stars in its cross-section, fire it, and your drawing opens in the sky with the same layout, then sags, drips and fades with the motion of a real burst.

Controls

  • Tap or drag on the shell to pack stars. Tap a star of the same colour to remove it, or pick the dashed eraser.
  • Choose Peony for crisp colour that twinkles out, or Willow for long golden trails that hang.
  • Press Fire shell (or F) to launch from the shore. Tap anywhere in the sky to burst it right there.
  • Patterns load a ready-made shell. Keys 1 to 6 pick a colour, E the eraser.
  • Sound starts after your first tap. The speaker button mutes it.

Inspired by

  • A tiny pixel-art editor by @duck_wtd on X: a microeditor where what you make comes alive straight away. Only that principle was taken.
  • Japanese pattern shells (kata-mono), where stars are arranged inside the shell to burst as smiles, hearts or rings.
  • Animal Crossing's custom design tool: a small editor whose result you immediately see out in the world.

For game UI

Creation tools feel better when the editor and the result share one material and one feedback loop. Make the canvas look like the thing it builds, keep the round trip from edit to payoff under two seconds, and let the payoff happen in the world, not in a preview box. The same idea fits emote makers, spell editors, banner and badge creators.
