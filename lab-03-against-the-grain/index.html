Against the Grain

Idea

What if you could groom the green before you putt? Real greens have grain: the grass leans one way, putts run faster with it and drift towards it. Here you are the greenkeeper. Brush a line into the turf and the ball follows what you combed. With the grain the grass reads light, against it reads dark.

Controls

  • Drag anywhere on the grass to brush it. A fast stroke lays it harder.
  • Drag back from the ball and release to putt. The dots preview the first second of the roll.
  • Sand kills the roll. Water costs a stroke.
  • The ball flattens the grass it rolls over, so every putt leaves a track.
  • Re-mow (M) restores the original stripes. Reset (R) puts the ball back.

Inspired by

  • "Plush Frog" by @N1njaCodex: a plush toy whose velvet darkens when you brush it the wrong way. Only the principle came along: a material that reads and behaves differently depending on the direction of your stroke.
  • Reading grain on real putting greens: shiny with the grain, dull against it, and the ball breaks towards where the grass grows.
  • Ōkami's Celestial Brush, where you paint on the world to change how it works.

For game UI

Let players edit the field, not only the shot. A surface with direction (wind, current, conveyor, grain) turns aiming into planning. Shade that direction, light with it and dark against it, and the rule becomes readable without arrows or tooltips. It fits flow puzzles, river levels and tower defense paths.
